Week 2 ended with three promises: port over a project, give the conveyor belt its first icon, and maybe, just maybe, bring back the blues.

All three happened (yay!!!)

## A neighbor for the shelf

[ContextGuard](/projects/contextguard/) is a VS Code extension I built and shipped in the weeks before this site went public: a linter for the AI configuration layer (the CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md files that agents read on every request and nobody reviews). This week it moved onto the shelf, with a project page, a post on [why it exists](/blog/note-2-why-i-built-a-linter-for-ai-instruction-files), and a [retro](/blog/note-3-contextguard-a-retro-after-the-fact) on how the build actually went.

The interesting problem was structural. Launch Start is built around sprints with weekly logs, and ContextGuard predates all of that. The first draft modeled it as a retroactive sprint, and it felt like filling out a form about the past with numbers nobody was tracking at the time. So there is a rule now: a project that arrives already shipped gets a retro post instead of a pretend sprint, and its story gets documented properly from here on.

The part that took longer than expected was not the prose. It was the numbers. Every figure I wanted to cite (file counts, dates, survey stats) got traced back to something checkable: git history, the changelog, the live Marketplace listing. ContextGuard once had to publicly correct its own survey figures, so this time nothing shipped without a receipt.

And the conveyor belt in the masthead finally carries an icon instead of drifting along empty. One icon on the belt. More to come.

## The blues are back!

Week 1 has a whole section about me cutting blue from the palette because I could not make it work. It stung a little...

This week blue returned, with a season attached. The site now has a winter theme: bright blue-white grounds, slate ink, blue accents where the pink used to be, and actual snowflakes drifting through the margins on wide screens. It is picked from a small theme dropdown in the nav (light, winter, dark), it remembers your choice, and it never overrides anyone who never touches it.

The token discipline from week 1 keeps paying rent. The entire theme is one block of CSS variables, checked against the same contrast ratios as the warm palette. The plan is a palette per season, and each new one should cost about an afternoon.

## What I learned

Structure earns its keep twice. The retro-not-sprint rule keeps the sprint system honest, and the token system made a second light palette almost free.

Receipts beat memory. The fastest way to write a wrong number is to remember it. Every stat in this week's posts came from an artifact I could point at, and the writing was better for it.
