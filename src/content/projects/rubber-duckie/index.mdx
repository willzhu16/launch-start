## What & why

Rubber Duckie is the other half of [Duckie](/projects/duckie/).

Duckie (the one on this site) explains my work to whoever visits. Rubber Duckie lives on my own computer and does the opposite - she listens while I explain my code to her.

That's the original rubber duck idea. When you're stuck, you talk through the problem line by line, and somewhere in the middle you find the bug yourself. Rubber Duckie keeps all of that, except now the duck can ask the question you skipped.

She's not a coding agent, and that's on purpose. She doesn't read my files, doesn't see my screen, and definitely doesn't write the fix. Tell her about a loop that never ends and she'll ask what's supposed to stop it. If I actually want ideas, there's a mode for that.

The longer story is in [why my rubber duck talks back now](/blog/note-6-why-my-rubber-duck-talks-back-now/).

## How it works

Same duck, same box, same animations. Rubber Duckie shares her body with the website Duckie, but what she says is completely separate. She doesn't get the portfolio notes or any of the website's limits, so she'll talk through pretty much any software problem.

Everything runs locally. The language model runs on my machine through llama.cpp, speech recognition is Faster Whisper (offline), and her voice is Kokoro's Heart, also local. No hosted fallback, no analytics, and no saved history. Conversations only live in memory and are gone as soon as the page closes.

The part I actually wrote is the gateway in the middle: the modes, the turn-taking, the mic rules, and what the page is and isn't allowed to do. Everything it talks to is swappable. The brain is just a setting, so I can point it at a different model whenever a better (and importantly, small) one comes out. Speech recognition already works with either Faster Whisper or whisper.cpp. And her voice sits behind one function, so trying a different voice engine means writing a small adapter instead of rebuilding everything else.

She has three modes: Rubber duck (mostly questions), Debug together (suggestions when I want them), and Just chat. I can type, record a thought and check the transcript before sending it, or turn on Talk mode - speak, pause, and she answers out loud. The mic only turns on when I ask, and it switches off while she's talking.

## Before the sprint

The first few days of work were the build: a local workbench, streaming conversation, mic input, local speech, a compact companion layout, and hands-free Talk mode. Right now it's all a browser preview. The rest of Sprint 1 is about turning her into an actual desktop app.
